# Khahare Eco Tours — Design Direction

This is the canonical art-direction brief for the Khahare Eco Tours rebuild. Use it to evaluate visual concepts, page composition, typography, imagery, interaction, and motion. For architecture, content strategy, migration, infrastructure, and operational constraints, refer to `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md` and `AGENTS.md`.

## Design north star

Khahare Eco Tours should feel like a world-class luxury expedition and hospitality brand based in Nepal.

The target perception is:

> This company must have spent serious money on its website.

The experience should feel bespoke, art-directed, cinematic, editorial, quiet but confident, sophisticated, immersive, human, natural, culturally grounded, and premium without appearing artificial.

The overall atmosphere is light-dominant: airy, warm, sunlit, calm, and connected to mountain light, stone, linen, hospitality, and natural daylight. Premium must not be interpreted as gloomy, black-heavy, or permanently moody. Deep forest and other dark surfaces are reserved for deliberate contrast and cinematic emphasis rather than used as the site's default field.

The existing WordPress website has zero visual authority over the new design. It must never feel like the output of a generic template or AI website prompt.

## Reference model

Use references according to the problem being solved:

- **Trip architecture and usability:** Intrepid Travel, G Adventures, and strong international adventure operators.
- **Premium storytelling and art direction:** &Beyond, Black Tomato, Explora, Aman-level hospitality restraint, premium expedition brands, and strong editorial travel publications.
- **Motion and interaction:** excellent GSAP-driven editorial websites and high-quality Awwwards-level interactive work where usability remains strong.

Do not copy the layout, brand language, or identity of any reference. The intended synthesis is:

```text
Aman / &Beyond restraint
+ Black Tomato emotional storytelling
+ Explora expedition atmosphere
+ Intrepid-quality trip architecture
+ sophisticated GSAP motion
```

The result must still belong unmistakably to Khahare Eco Tours and Nepal.

## Anti-AI-slop rules

Avoid patterns commonly associated with generic AI-generated websites:

- every section centered;
- a giant vague headline followed by three cards;
- endless rounded cards or excessive pill-shaped UI;
- glowing backgrounds, purple/blue SaaS gradients, or glassmorphism everywhere;
- random blobs or decorative grid backgrounds;
- fake numbers and statistics;
- generic icon rows;
- repetitive alternating image/text blocks;
- identical spacing and section structure throughout a page;
- generic “Discover your next adventure” wording;
- meaningless “Explore,” “Discover,” or “Experience” labels;
- stock luxury language without specific meaning;
- every element fading upward; and
- arbitrary animation applied to everything.

Do not make every section structurally similar. A page should feel composed, not assembled from a component library.

## Visual composition

Use editorial composition. Consider:

- asymmetric layouts;
- large negative space;
- intentional overlaps;
- full-bleed imagery;
- off-grid moments;
- unusual but purposeful image crops;
- dramatic changes in scale;
- controlled whitespace;
- text aligned against imagery rather than automatically placed inside cards;
- large typographic moments; and
- sections that deliberately break the normal container.

Not every section needs a visible box, background, or card. Photography should often become the layout itself. Treat whitespace as an active design element that creates focus, rhythm, and confidence.

## Typography

Typography should carry a substantial share of the premium character. Aim for:

- distinctive display typography;
- highly readable body text;
- disciplined hierarchy;
- generous line height;
- deliberate line lengths;
- occasional large editorial headlines; and
- careful uppercase and tracking for metadata only where justified.

Do not select fonts merely because they are fashionable. The eventual type system should suit Nepal, expedition travel, hospitality, editorial sophistication, and human warmth.

Avoid typography that makes the brand resemble SaaS, finance, cryptocurrency, futuristic technology, or generic corporate consulting. Final typefaces are not yet locked.

## Color

The final palette is not yet locked. It should be natural, grounded, sophisticated, restrained, and inspired by Nepal and its landscapes.

Warm paper, pale mineral, linen, and sunlit stone should form the dominant surface hierarchy. Potential supporting families include deep forest, dark mineral tones, earth, muted moss, and occasional culturally meaningful accents where their use is justified.

The site must remain light-dominant without becoming beige, flat, or bland. Visual tension should come from typography, composition, scale, imagery, and selective contrast. Deep forest remains an important brand color, but primarily as an accent, text color, image tone, or occasional special-section surface. Dark sections should be rare enough to feel intentional.

Avoid black-heavy luxury styling, dark hotel-lounge atmosphere, nightclub mood, neon, overly saturated palettes, generic technology gradients, and fake metallic gold used as a shortcut for luxury. Premium character must come from execution rather than stereotypical “luxury colors.”

## Photography

Photography is one of the site's most important design materials. Prefer:

- landscapes that communicate scale;
- genuine human moments;
- trekking and expedition experiences;
- villages, culture, and hospitality;
- nature and close environmental details;
- atmospheric light; and
- authentic local experience.

Avoid obvious stock imagery, staged corporate tourism photographs, oversaturated travel images, and pictures selected merely to fill a card.

Treat images editorially through full-bleed placement, portrait and panoramic crops, edge crops, controlled zoom, layering, masks, and transitions. Every major image needs a visual and narrative purpose.

## Motion language

Motion should make the website feel expensive: confident, smooth, deliberate, choreographed, and physically coherent. It should be slow enough to read as premium and fast enough to keep the interface responsive.

Use motion selectively through:

- masked reveals;
- image clipping;
- staggered typography;
- subtle scale changes;
- parallax;
- pinned scenes;
- scroll-linked sequences;
- layered movement;
- changing image states;
- elegant page transitions;
- refined hover motion; and
- cursor interaction only when it is genuinely useful.

Avoid bouncing, elastic gimmicks, random rotation, constant movement, universal fade-up effects, overlong introductions, delayed interaction, scroll hijacking, and excessive inertia.

Apply motion through a clear hierarchy:

1. Page-level motion
2. Section-level motion
3. Component-level motion
4. Micro-interactions
5. Cinematic hero moments

Not every layer needs to activate simultaneously. Choreography should create focus rather than competition.

## Homepage experience direction

The following is an experiential direction, not a locked wireframe or component checklist. Design the homepage from first principles.

### 1. Cinematic opening

Use fullscreen Nepal photography or a short ambient film, minimal navigation, statement-level typography, and a controlled introduction sequence.

### 2. Brand and place introduction

Establish Nepal and Khahare Eco Tours emotionally through editorial composition and genuine human or local grounding.

### 3. Signature journeys

Avoid a generic grid of equal-size cards. Favor large image-led storytelling, allow different journeys to use different compositions, and make transitions between them feel cinematic.

### 4. Story and philosophy

Express local expertise, responsible travel, meaningful travel, and community connection through photography and concise, specific writing—not icon boxes.

### 5. Immersive editorial moment

Create a strong scroll-driven sequence around landscape, culture, or people. This may become one of the homepage's primary cinematic moments.

### 6. Practical trip discovery

Present duration, difficulty, location, and trip type through clear information architecture inspired by serious adventure operators while maintaining the premium visual language.

### 7. Trust and reviews

Use a restrained editorial presentation. Do not default to a carousel unless it is demonstrably the strongest solution.

### 8. Stay and other experiences

Use Airbnb, stays, or other relevant experiences as a lifestyle-oriented visual break where they support the story.

### 9. Closing moment

Finish with emotionally strong imagery, a clear enquiry action, and a confident premium composition.

## Trip detail page direction

Trip pages must balance immersion with practical usability. They may include:

- a cinematic destination hero;
- critical trip facts visible early;
- a strong editorial introduction;
- large-scale photography;
- route and itinerary storytelling;
- sticky or scroll-linked scenes where justified;
- complete trip facts;
- inclusions and exclusions;
- practical information;
- a gallery;
- related journeys; and
- a clear enquiry action.

Do not automatically reduce the itinerary to a generic accordion. Explore more editorial ways to communicate the route while ensuring that every detail remains accessible.

Trekking, cultural tours, and other experience types may use different page treatments when the variation improves storytelling and usability.

## Navigation

Navigation should feel premium and deliberate rather than like a generic Bootstrap navbar. Viable directions include minimal desktop navigation, an immersive fullscreen menu, elegant menu transitions, relevant image or destination previews, and restrained sticky behavior.

Whatever its visual treatment, navigation must remain obvious, keyboard accessible, responsive, and fast.

## Mobile direction

Mobile is a first-class design target, not a collapsed desktop layout. Recompose layouts and reconsider content order, image crops, navigation, spacing, typography, pacing, and touch interactions for narrow screens.

Reduce or change motion where appropriate. Avoid complex pinned scenes that become awkward or fragile on small screens. The mobile experience should retain the same level of art direction through proportion and pacing rather than attempting to reproduce every desktop effect.

## Design review questions

Before accepting a major page or section, ask:

1. Does this look custom or template-generated?
2. Does it look like an AI-designed site?
3. Could this exact section belong to 500 other travel websites?
4. Is the photography doing real design work?
5. Does the typography feel considered?
6. Is there enough negative space?
7. Does the animation improve the experience?
8. Is anything moving only because GSAP exists?
9. Does this feel like a luxury expedition brand?
10. Does it remain clearly connected to Nepal and Khahare Eco Tours?
11. Is mobile receiving real design attention?
12. Would removing an element make the section stronger?

If a design feels generic, redesign it rather than polishing the generic version.

## Working rule

Do not accept the first plausible layout. Major visual sections should be iterated.

For each major page or section:

1. Establish hierarchy.
2. Establish composition.
3. Establish typography.
4. Establish imagery.
5. Design motion around the finished composition.

Do not use animation to rescue weak static design. The static composition should already look exceptional before animation is added.
